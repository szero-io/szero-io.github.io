Control model / Human in command

Autonomy can expand human agency.

People cannot inspect every price, allocation, control action, experiment, or operational choice when decisions occur at high volume, speed, or complexity.

Human control moves to the system level. People set the purpose, boundaries, and evidence required. The system executes individual decisions only within that authority.

Intent is authorized, not unilateral.

Human intent means authorized intent under explicit stakeholder, legal, safety, and ethical constraints. It is not blind obedience to one operator.

01

Humans define intent

Authorized people define constraints, authority, acceptable outcomes, escalation rules, and promotion criteria.

02

Oversight follows risk

Direct approval or supervision depends on the action, environment, risk, and system maturity.

03

The system executes

The system makes individual decisions within an evaluated autonomy envelope. Unfamiliar or higher-impact actions can still require human approval.

Autonomy envelope / Authorization in practice

Authority is a boundary, not a switch.

The autonomy envelope defines where the system may operate, what it may decide, and when it must reduce authority or return control.

Evidence can expand the envelope. Drift, uncertainty, violations, or failures can reduce it.

Evidence / Authority earned

Evaluate before execution. Assure during operation.

Autonomy grows through connected layers of evidence, deployment, assurance, and learning.

Evaluation measures performance and alignment. Runtime controls watch the live boundary. Outcomes become knowledge for the next decision, without allowing learned behavior to bypass review.

01 / EVALUATE

Layered evaluation

Replay, simulation, counterfactuals, adversarial tests, domain review, constraints, and baseline comparisons test both decision quality and alignment.

02 / DEPLOY

Staged deployment

Authority increases only through explicit stages and promotion evidence appropriate to the domain and risk.

03 / ASSURE

Runtime assurance

Monitor authority limits, drift, uncertainty, affected populations, interventions, failures, recovery, and outcome quality.

04 / LEARN

Learning from outcomes

Retain state, evidence, decisions, actions, interventions, failures, and outcomes so operation produces inspectable, reusable knowledge.
